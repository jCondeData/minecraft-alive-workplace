# M40 design note: the Village Hall, simpler

Written for ROADMAP 40.1 (2026-10-26) before any code. 40.2 to 40.4 (the People table, Job ▾ and Bed ▾) build on the
sections laid out here.

**In one line:** the hall's screen gets a left sidebar of ten labeled buttons (an item icon and a word each), with the
village's name and "Spring, day 1 · Year 1" above them. The chosen section is highlighted and fills the right side;
nothing of today's screen is lost, every button and page moves under one section (some under two).

## How it works

- The server lays out the hall's main page exactly as before (same slots, same clicks), so every page, every back
  button and every test keeps working. It also marks each main-page button with the section(s) it belongs to
  (`HallSections`, a mark in the icon's custom data) and tells the client the open section, the date
  and which sections are empty (five numbers on the hall's menu).
- The client draws only the open section's buttons, each as a labeled row (icon, name, first line), and the pane on
  the right reads out what's under the mouse as today. A page opened from a section (the chronicle, the Defense page,
  ...) fills the right side with the sidebar still there; a sidebar button always goes back to that section's list.
- A sidebar click is vanilla's menu-button click (`clickMenuButton`, the enchanting table's), so no new packet.
- The last section a player opened is remembered for that player while the server (or single-player world) runs; a
  new hall screen opens on it. Default: Overview.
- A section with nothing in it says so in a plain sentence at the top of its list ("Nobody lives here yet. ...").
  Its buttons (the Book of Edicts, the recall bell, ...) stay below, so there's always something to do.
- Pages registered by other features (`HallPages.register`) go under the section `HallSections.place` names for
  them; a page with none goes under Overview, never dropped.

Only Overview is never empty. The Overview section's list is the village's "At a glance" figures (the nine figures of
row 0) and "What next?"; its pane, with nothing under the mouse, reads out the advice tips. The other sections' panes
say in one line what the section is for.

Sections and their sidebar icons: Overview (bell), People (emerald), Homes & Food (red bed), Buildings (bricks),
Defense (iron sword), Quests (map), Trade (chest minecart), Calendar (clock), Laws & Realm (lectern), Map (filled map).
All vanilla items, so no new art.

**Empty sections** (a plain sentence on top): People with nobody living there; Homes & Food with no beds and no food;
Buildings with nothing going up; Defense with no guards; Quests with none open; Trade with no village near enough;
Laws & Realm with no edict in force and no realm. Calendar and Map always have something.

## Decisions

- Keep the server's slots: the screen is a client view over the same 54 buttons. Rewriting every page for a new
  protocol would have meant rewriting 30-odd pages and 90 test files, with nothing gained for players.
- One mark per icon with a bit per section, so a button can sit in two sections (the recall bell is a People and a
  Defense thing; the Steward's desk plans buildings and gives advice).
- Remembered per player on the server (not a client static): the server picks what to lay out, and a remembered
  section has to survive reopening from a Village Ledger far away. It is not saved: a restart opens Overview.
- At GUI scale 4 on 1280×720 (320×180) the sidebar's rows shrink to 11 pixels with icons drawn smaller, and the date
  wraps under the name.
- 40.2, the People table: the server's 25-person window stays; the table scrolls over everyone and moves the window
  with a menu-button click (1000 + the first person), each person's button carries the table's columns in its custom
  data, and the counts are five more menu numbers. A row opens client-side (kept by the villager's id), showing the
  person's hall tooltip lines. Job ▾ opens today's Change job (36.18) or free jobs page and Bed ▾ the Homes & Food
  section until 40.3 and 40.4 replace them. Children are added to the list, last.
- 40.3, Job ▾: the list of every job rides in the open person's button (a container component, one stack per line with
  its name, tooltip and a mark), opened, picked and closed with menu-button clicks (100000 + slot, 200000 + the job's
  registry number, 199999), so still no new packet. It reuses 36.18's retraining for workers; a jobless villager's
  first job is free, and No job is free with two clicks.
- 40.4, Bed ▾: the village's beds ride in the open person's button the same way (300000 + slot, 400000 + the line's
  number, 399999). Choosing a bed uses vanilla's own home: the bed's point of interest taken and the `HOME` memory set,
  the old bed released; a swap trades two `HOME` memories. Nothing new is saved.
