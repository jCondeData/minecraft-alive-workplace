# M39 design note: Create integration (2.3)

Written for ROADMAP 39.1 (2026-10-18). The roadmap items 39.2 to 39.7 hold the full specs and their tests; this note
is the overview the owner reviews and the map the Create lane builds from. Lanes don't wait for the owner's reply;
anything he changes goes back into the items.

**In one line:** villages go mechanical with Create: a **Mechanic** keeps the village's machines turning and mends
their shafts, cogwheels and belts; villagers **feed Create's machines** (the farmer's millstone, the smith's press, the
chef's mixer, the miner's crushing wheels) and bring what they make to the store; builders raise a **Watermill**, a
**Windmill** and a **Workshop**, build **working contraptions** from a blueprint, and import **Create schematics** at
the Blueprint Table; later, **trains** link villages for caravans.

Everything here needs Create: the owner's Fabric port, `create-fabric-6.0.10.0+mc1.21.1.jar` (mod id `create`, MIT,
in `libs/`). Without it there is no Mechanic, no machine work, no Create builds and no schematics at the table, and
caravans walk as today. Nothing breaks and nothing is logged as an error when it's missing. It ships as **2.3**,
behind `Expansions.M39` (off on `main` until the release that finishes the milestone).

## What you'll see (the short version)

1. **The Mechanic (39.2).** A jobless villager next to a **Toolbox** (Create's own, any colour) becomes the
   village's Mechanic, in a work apron with goggles on the forehead and a wrench at the belt. Through the day they
   walk a round of the village's machines: a millstone that's full gets emptied into the store, an empty one gets
   wheat; a shaft, cogwheel or belt that's gone from a finished build is put back from the build's blueprint, the way
   builders mend a build today. When builders put up a contraption (4 below) the Mechanic leads the crew.
2. **Villagers work the machines (39.3).** The farmer takes wheat from the store to the millstone and brings back
   flour; the armorer feeds iron ingots under the press and brings back iron sheets; the chef fills the mixer's basin
   with flour and water and takes out dough; the miner drops raw ore onto the crushing wheels and gathers the crushed
   ore. They only work a machine while it turns, stop when the store is full, and carry on after a restart.
3. **Create-powered builds (39.4).** Three new blueprints at the Blueprint Table, only with Create installed: the
   **Watermill** (a water wheel turning a millstone, by a stream), the **Windmill** (a windmill bearing with sails,
   started by the builder when it's done) and the **Workshop** (a press over a depot on a belt line, driven by a water
   wheel). Each is the workplace of a machine job above.
4. **Contraptions from a blueprint (39.5).** A blueprint with Create's moving parts in it (a player's own, or one of
   ours) is built in an order that keeps it whole: what holds it up, then shafts and gearboxes, then cogwheels, then
   belts. Each part keeps its axis and facing, and belts keep their length. When it's done the builder checks that it
   turns and lists, on the build's tooltip, whatever doesn't.
5. **Create schematics at the table (39.6).** The Blueprint Table also lists the schematics players have uploaded to
   the server with Create's Schematic Table, and a written Schematic right-clicked on the table imports its file.
   Blocks the server doesn't have are counted and named, as for other imports.
6. **Trains link villages (39.7, later).** A **Train Station** build, track laid by builders along the caravan road,
   and caravans that take the train between two villages with stations instead of walking.

No `compat/create/` class is planned. Everything goes by block, item and recipe-type ids, block state names, block
entity data read by name, data component ids, Fabric's item transfer (Create's port exposes its inventories through
it, which `ItemStores` already uses) and plain vanilla calls. Should 39.3 to 39.7 find something that can't be done
that way, a `compat/create/` class with `modCompileOnly(files("libs/..."))` is the fallback, said in that item's Notes.

### The Mechanic's job block: Create's Toolbox

`create:brown_toolbox` (the colour a toolbox is crafted in) and its fifteen dyed versions, `create:white_toolbox` to
`create:black_toolbox`. Why the toolbox:

- **It's a mechanic's thing.** Create's own tool-carrying block; a villager standing at one reads at a glance.
- **It's cheap and early.** Players have one in the first hour of Create; no rotation, no brass.
- **It's never a machine.** The millstone, press, mixer, basin and depot are workplaces of other jobs (39.3) and
  parts of builds and contraptions; a POI on them would let a Mechanic claim a mill. A toolbox is never part of a
  drive train, so placing machines never makes job sites by accident.
- **It's one block, not a full cube.** It sits on a bench or a floor like the other job blocks.
- Every colour counts, so a dyed toolbox doesn't stop being a workplace. The villager never opens it or takes
  anything from it; a player still can.

Registration: the profession is registered at our init only when `isModLoaded("create")`, so without Create there is
no Mechanic (39.2's test). The POI needs Create's block objects, and Fabric doesn't promise that Create's initializer
runs before ours, so `Platform` gets a "when these block ids are registered" hook (Fabric's
`RegistryEntryAddedCallback` on the block registry, or at once if they're already there) that registers the POI.
Both happen before the registries freeze.

### The machine-work data format (39.3)

**`data/aliveworkplace/machine_work/<id>.json`**: one file per machine job. A datapack can add or replace them. Files
whose `requires` mod isn't loaded, or whose machine block doesn't exist, are skipped quietly.

```json
{
  "requires": "create",
  "machine": "create:millstone",
  "job": "minecraft:farmer",
  "recipe_type": "create:milling",
  "feed": {"at": [0, 0, 0]},
  "collect": {"at": [0, 0, 0]},
  "makes": [{"item": "create:wheat_flour", "keep": 64}],
  "batch": 16,
  "min_speed": 1
}
```

- `machine`: the block the job is about; the worker's workplace is that block in a finished build (or any found
  within the village's reach).
- `job`: the profession that works it (vanilla or ours). The Mechanic may work any machine whose own worker is
  missing.
- `recipe_type`: Create's recipe type by id. What a machine makes is read from those recipes through the recipe
  manager: the worker picks a recipe of that type whose result is in `makes` and whose ingredients the store holds.
  No recipe is written into our files, so a datapack that changes Create's recipes changes the village's too.
- `feed` and `collect`: where the input goes and the output comes out, as an offset from the machine, optionally with
  the block expected there (`"block": "create:depot"`), and `"drop": true` for a spot the worker drops items into or
  picks dropped items up from (the crushing wheels' gap, the floor under them). Inventories are reached through
  `ItemStores`.
- `makes`: what the village wants made, each with how many the store should keep; nothing is fed once there are that
  many, or when the store is full.
- `batch`: the most the worker carries per trip. `min_speed`: the machine counts as turning when the absolute value
  of its block entity's `Speed` (read by name from its saved data) is at least this many RPM; below that nothing is
  fed.

The four files 39.3 ships:

| File | Machine | Job | Recipe type | Feed | Collect | Makes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `millstone.json` | `create:millstone` | farmer | `create:milling` | the millstone | the millstone | `create:wheat_flour` |
| `press.json` | `create:mechanical_press` | armorer | `create:pressing` | a depot below (`[0,-2,0]`) | the depot | `create:iron_sheet`, `create:copper_sheet` |
| `mixer.json` | `create:mechanical_mixer` | `aliveworkplace:chef` | `create:mixing` | a basin below (`[0,-2,0]`), with a water bucket's worth | the basin | `create:dough` |
| `crushing.json` | `create:crushing_wheel_controller` | `aliveworkplace:miner` | `create:crushing` | drop into the gap (`[0,0,0]`) | drop below (`[0,-1,0]`) | `create:crushed_raw_iron`, `_copper`, `_gold` |

(The mixer's dough recipe, `create:mixing/dough_by_mixing`, takes wheat flour and 81,000 droplets of water: the chef
pours a water bucket into the basin through `ItemStores`' fluid side, or the file names `minecraft:water_bucket` as an
extra input the chef empties there. 39.3 decides which works with the port, and says so in its Notes.)

### The builds (39.4, 39.7)

| Build | What's in it | Workplace of | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Watermill | a `create:water_wheel` on a shaft into a `create:millstone`, a water channel, a store chest | the farmer's millstone | By flowing water; the build's water is placed last so the wheel turns as soon as it's done. |
| Windmill | a `create:windmill_bearing` with `create:white_sail` and `create:sail_frame` sails (at least 8), a shaft down to a millstone | the farmer's millstone | Started by the builder (below). |
| Workshop | a `create:water_wheel`, shafts, `create:cogwheel`s, a `create:belt` line, a `create:mechanical_press` over a `create:depot` | the armorer's press | The belt is placed with `create:belt_connector`'s block states and data (39.5). |
| Train Station (39.7) | a `create:track_station` on a straight `create:track` run, a platform and a roof | the caravan's stop | Later item. |

Each is drawn with the architect skill and checked against `tools/blueprints/STYLE.md` in a render; each is listed
at the table only with Create installed (`"requires": ["create"]` in its `blueprint_unlocks` entry).

**Starting the windmill without Create's classes.** Read from the port: `WindmillBearingBlock.useItemOn` assembles the
sails when a player who may build uses it with an **empty hand, not sneaking** (it queues the assembly for the bearing's
next tick). That's vanilla's `BlockState.useItemOn(ItemStack.EMPTY, level, player, hand, hit)` on the bearing, which
any mod can call. So the builder, once the Windmill is done, uses the bearing as a Fabric fake player (a new
`Platform.fakePlayer(ServerLevel)`, standing at the builder) with an empty hand. The bearing then saves `Running` in
its block entity data, so it keeps turning after a reload. If it doesn't start (fewer than 8 sails left, the sails
blocked), the builder places a sign by the bearing: "Right-click the bearing with an empty hand to start the
windmill." 39.4's test reads the millstone's `Speed` to prove it turns.

### Contraptions (39.5)

The STRUCTURE stage sorts kinetic blocks after their supports: supports, then `create:shaft` and `create:gearbox`
(and encased shafts), then `create:cogwheel` and `create:large_cogwheel`, then `create:belt`. The order comes from a
list of block ids per group (data, so other Create add-ons' parts can be added), not from Create classes. Block states
(`axis`, `facing`) are copied as they are, as for every block today. Belts need their block entity data: Create's belt
saves `Controller` (an absolute position), `Index`, `Length`, `IsController` and `Casing`; the builder copies it with
`Controller` moved by the build's own offset and rotation, so the belt finds its controller in the new place. When the
build is done the builder reads each kinetic block's `Speed`; any at 0 are listed ("3 parts don't turn: cogwheel at
...").

### Schematics (39.6)

Create keeps uploaded schematics in `<server>/schematics/uploaded/<player>/<name>.nbt` (read from the port's
`CreatePaths`: the game folder's `schematics/uploaded`). They're structure `.nbt` files, which `BlueprintImporter`
already reads. A written `create:schematic` item holds its file name in the data component `create:schematic_file`
and its uploader in `create:schematic_owner` (both strings, read by component id, no Create class), so a right-click
on the table imports `schematics/uploaded/<owner>/<file>`. A missing file says so in chat.

### Fallbacks

- **Create not installed:** no Mechanic profession, no toolbox POI, the `machine_work` files are skipped, the three
  builds and the station aren't at the table, the table lists no schematics and right-clicking with anything does what
  it does today, caravans walk. No error is logged.
- **Create removed from a world that had it:** Create's blocks are gone (Minecraft's own behaviour). A saved Mechanic's
  profession no longer exists; vanilla reads that villager's data as a jobless one (39.2 tests this). Machine records
  whose block is gone are dropped; build steps with unknown blocks count as skipped, as for any import.
- **A machine that doesn't turn:** nothing is fed; the Mechanic's round says so in the hall's chronicle once a day at
  most ("The millstone by the river has stopped").
- **The windmill won't start:** the sign (above).
- **M27 off (caravan roads):** no track to lay; stations stand but caravans walk. **M33 off (caravans):** no trains
  to ride.

### How the port runs in our tests

Found while getting it to load next to the compat suite's other mods (148 mods load in all) (`runCompatGameTest`, 236 of 236 tests
passing with it on 2026-10-18):

- **From the `mods/` folder, not the classpath.** The jar goes into the compat suite's `mods/` folder
  (`tests.compat_files`) and, with `-Pcreate=true` (`CREATE=true` for `tools/screenshots/run.sh`, or a scene's
  `create=True` in `tools/showcase/scenes.py`), into the screenshot client's. Fabric Loader remaps it at launch and picks
  one version of each library it nests, as in a real install. The other optional mods are on the classpath with their
  nested jars flattened (`nestedJars`); that can't work for this jar, which nests three versions of Porting Lib's
  core, two of Flywheel and two of MixinExtras inside its own libraries.
- **Sophisticated Core's old Porting Lib.** Sophisticated Core nests Porting Lib 3.1.0-beta.47. Flattened onto the
  classpath it became a root mod, which the loader takes over Create's beta.90, and Create stopped at start-up
  (`NoClassDefFoundError: ...porting_lib/core/event/entity/EntityEvent`). `nestedJars` now leaves out any nested
  library that a `mods/` jar of the same run nests at the same or a newer version, so the loader picks beta.90 for both.
- **Porting Lib against owo-lib, in dev only.** Porting Lib's `ItemStackMixin` soft-implements `set`, `remove` and
  `applyComponents` with `@Intrinsic(displace = true)`. In a real install vanilla's methods have intermediary names
  (`method_57366` and so on), nothing meets and nothing is displaced. In our Mojang-named dev runtime the names are the
  same, so vanilla's `applyComponents` was displaced, and owo-lib's injection into it (owo is here for Accessories and
  Mega Showdown) stopped the game ("cannot inject into ... merged by ...ItemStackMixin"). The test copy turns that
  class's `displace` to false (a small ASM edit in `build.gradle.kts`, `keepIntrinsicTargets`), so vanilla's method,
  which in dev already is the interface's method, stays. A real server is unaffected; nothing in the shipped mod
  changes. (Raising or lowering the mixin's priority didn't help: merging always comes before injecting.)
- **Their own GameTests left out.** The copy drops the `fabric-gametest` entrypoints of the jar and of every jar it
  nests: Create's own suite (19 test classes) and Porting Lib's test need their own test structures, and are theirs to run.
  `CreateCompatTests.createIsLoaded` checks they stay out and that Porting Lib's core is beta.90.
- Forge Config API Port: Create nests 21.1.3, the compat runtime already has 21.1.6 (for Sophisticated Core), the
  loader keeps the newer one, which Create accepts (`>=21.1.3`). Flywheel, Create's renderer, loads only on a client.
- It's never a `depends` of the mod: `create` is in `suggests`, and only the compat test mod depends on it.

### Other decisions taken (not the owner's)

- **The Toolbox is the Mechanic's job block** (above).
- **No Miller, Presser or Mixer job:** the jobs that already make those goods work the machines (the farmer mills, the
  armorer presses, the chef mixes, the miner crushes), so a village with Create needs no new villagers beyond the
  Mechanic.
- **What a machine makes comes from Create's recipes**, never our own list, so a datapack or an add-on that changes
  them changes the village's too.
- **A machine is "turning" at 1 RPM or more** (`min_speed`, per file): a large water wheel turns slower than a small
  one, and any speed at all lets Create's machines work, only more slowly.
- **The windmill is started by a fake player's empty-hand use**, with a sign as the fallback.
- **No Create classes**, so the mod keeps compiling without the port; the port is only a test and screenshot runtime.
