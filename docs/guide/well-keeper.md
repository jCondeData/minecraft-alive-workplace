The Well Keeper looks after the village's water: the cauldrons, the farmland and, when the rain stays away, the crops
themselves.

## Getting a Well Keeper

Stand a villager by a **cauldron** and sneak-right-click them with **any shovel**. Leather at the same cauldron still
makes a [Leatherworker](leatherworker-dyer.md), and the cauldron's other jobs keep their own items. The
[Well House](well-house-build.md) is built around a Well Keeper's cauldron.

## At work

The Well Keeper works the long day shift.

- **In a [drought](drought.md)** they fill a bucket at the nearest well, river or pond within 24 blocks of their
  cauldron and carry it to the fields. Each bucket soaks the nine farmland blocks round where it lands, and the crops
  on them grow at their full pace for the rest of the day, drought or not.
- **Between droughts** they keep the village's water cauldrons full and wet any farmland that has dried right out.
- **They read the sky.** With a Well Keeper in the village, you hear of a drought a day before it strikes: "Rowan looks
  at the wells and the sky: a drought will come to Oakvale."

Each bucket earns them experience. Over their head you can see whether their bucket is full and where they're carrying
it.

## How it fits in

- **What feeds it:** open water within 24 blocks of the cauldron, and fields close to it. A Well Keeper whose cauldron
  is far from water spends the day walking.
- **What it protects:** the [Farmer](farmer.md)'s wheat, carrots, potatoes and beetroots, melon and pumpkin stems and
  berry bushes, which grow at a quarter of their pace in a drought, and so the village's food.
- **Helped by:** Water-type Pokémon in a Pasture Block near the cauldron (with Cobblemon): they fill and pour each
  bucket faster.
- **The hall knows:** a village with no Well Keeper gets a tip in **What next?** to build the Well House.
- **What stops it:** **Well Keepers** off in the settings screen. Well Keepers already hired then stand idle, and the
  cauldron no longer offers the job.

## Trading

Well Keepers buy a bucket and sell a water bucket. Higher up they trade in clay, a cauldron, cobblestone and pointed
dripstone, and at the top a wet sponge.
