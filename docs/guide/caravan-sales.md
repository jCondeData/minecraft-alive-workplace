Your caravans have always taken a partner village what its workers were waiting for, free. With the economy they also
go to market.

## What goes

Each caravan on a route takes up to **2 more stacks** of goods your village is **known for** (the gold stars on the
[price board](price-board.md)) to a village that is **short of** them, or that pays at least 10% more for them than
yours does. The other village's treasury pays yours at its board's price.

## Seeing it

On the hall's Trade page, the **Routes** tab says what a caravan sells in every village in range:

- with the route on: "Our caravan sells there:" and a line for each stack, such as two stacks of Timber and what
  they would fetch today;
- without a route: "A caravan would sell there:", so you can see which route would pay before you open it.

When the caravan arrives, both chronicles tell it: yours says what was sold to Ashford and for how much,
theirs what was bought from Thornholm. The emeralds are in your [treasury](treasury.md).

If the other village's treasury can't pay for everything, it buys what it can and the rest comes home to your
[Storehouse](storehouse.md): "Our caravan came home from Ashford with 16 × Oak Log unsold".

## How it fits in

- **What feeds it:** [trade routes](trade-and-caravans.md), and a village known for something: workers of a making job,
  the right land, a full Storehouse. See [Economy](economy.md).
- **Faster and bigger with:** the realm's **Royal Roads** research and the [Royal Highway](royal-highway.md) edict
  between members of a [realm](realms.md).
- **With other players:** a route to another player's village needs a [trade pact](trade-pacts.md), and then even what
  their workers wait for is paid for.
- **What stops it:** **Village Economy** off: caravans carry only what the other village waits for, and goods on the
  road come home unsold.
