# M34 design note: Classes and luxuries (1.8)

Written for ROADMAP 34.1 (2026-10-05). The roadmap items 34.2 to 34.23 hold the full specs and their tests; this note
is the overview the owner reviews and the map the lanes build from. Lanes don't wait for the owner's reply; anything
he changes goes back into the items.

**In one line:** every household in a village with a hall climbs a four-step ladder (Peasant, Artisan, Burgher, Noble)
by having a good house, good food, services nearby and luxuries from four new trades; higher classes pay more tax,
unlock higher jobs, dress the part and want grander houses, while grown villagers age into elders who hand their job
to the next generation, and every family keeps a tree.

## What you'll see (the short version)

- **A class for everyone.** Each villager's line on the hall says their class and household ("Burgher · married to
  Tomas"). A **household** is one grown villager on their own, or a married couple; children belong to the grown-ups
  who sleep in their house. Everyone starts as a Peasant, the floor nobody falls below.
- **Rising at dawn.** When a household has had everything the next class needs for **2 dawns running**, they rise one
  step at the next dawn: golden sparkles at their door, a chime, a chat line to players within 32 blocks, a chronicle
  entry and "rose in the world" (+10 mood for 2 days). If they lack something their own class needs for **3 dawns
  running**, they come down one step ("came down in the world", -10 for 2 days). One step a day at most.
- **Four new trades make the luxuries** the higher classes want: the **Vintner** (cider and wines, at a cauldron), the
  **Tailor** (clothes, at a loom), the **Printer** (books, the village newspaper and illuminated books, at a
  cartography table) and the **Jeweller** (rings, brooches and circlets, at a stonecutter). Porters carry their goods
  to the storehouse, and each household takes what it needs from the village store at dawn.
- **A Classes page on the hall** (a new button): how many households are in each class, what each class needs and
  gives ("Fine Clothes: 2 of 5"), and who is closest to rising and what they lack. **What next?** gets class tips
  ("4 artisan households want Berry Wine to become Burghers: a Vintner at a cauldron, Apprentice or better").
- **What a class gives:** Artisans and up pay more tax (Peasants pay exactly what everyone pays today, so no village
  earns less), Artisan crafters work 10% faster, Burgher scholars research 15% faster, three Burgher households open
  one more caravan route and a fourth market trader, and Nobles lift the whole village's wellbeing and hold a
  **Noble's Ball** in place of every other festival.
- **Higher jobs need higher classes**: a Scholar must be a Burgher, a Teacher an Artisan, and so on. Nobody is ever
  fired by this, and vanilla jobs (Farmer, Librarian, Mason…) are never gated.
- **Dressed by class.** Artisans, Burghers and Nobles wear a class layer over their job's outfit (a neckerchief, a
  white collar, a fur-trimmed mantle…); the job's hat and the level badge stay readable. Peasants look exactly as
  today. You pick from three versions of each on a picker page (34.17).
- **Grander homes:** the Artisan's House, the Burgher's Townhouse and the Noble's Manor (with a ballroom wing for the
  ball). A household that outgrows its home asks for a better one in What next?.
- **Elders.** After 120 grown days a villager is an elder: grey brows or a beard, a slower walk, "a quiet old age" in
  their mood. An elder with a job retires when someone can take over: their grown child (or another jobless villager)
  shadows them for 2 days, then takes the workstation one level lower; the retired elder then mentors young workers
  of their old trade. **Elders never die of old age** unless you say so (`elderPassing`, off).
- **Family trees.** Every villager gets a family name shown on the hall ("Dara (Ashby)"), never on their name tag.
  A **Family** button shows parents, grandparents, partner, brothers and sisters, children and grandchildren; the
  Printer prints any family's tree as a book for 2 emeralds.

### The class ladder (34.2, 34.7, 34.8)

"Within 48" means within 48 blocks of the household's home (34.3). Luxuries are taken from the village store; "every
8 days" means one each 8 days.

| Class | Tax | Needs (all must hold) | Wants (each one: +10% tax) | Jobs only this class or higher may take |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Peasant** (tier 0) | ×1 | none: everyone's floor | none | Vintner, Tailor and every job not listed below |
| **Artisan** (tier 1) | ×1.5 | a bed in a house a builder built (grade I+); fed 3 days running; a varied diet; a chapel, school or clinic within 48; Work Clothes every 8 days | Cider every 4 days; a tavern within 48 | Tinkerer, Chef, Netherworker, Nurse, Teacher, Shopkeeper, Innkeeper, Printer; with Cobblemon: Ball Smith, Move Tutor, Pokémon Trader, Fossil Scientist |
| **Burgher** (tier 2) | ×2.5 | a grade II+ home; the Artisan's food and diet; a school and one more service within 48; a Market Square in the village; Fine Clothes every 8 days; Berry Wine every 2 days; the Gazette every 7 days | an Amethyst Ring every 16 days; a library within 48; beauty 2+ near home | Scholar, Undertaker, Jeweller, Trainer Leader |
| **Noble** (tier 3) | ×4 | a grade III+ home; a chapel, a school, a clinic and a library within 48; the village a Town or City; beauty 3+ near home; Noble Robes every 8 days; Vintage Wine every 2 days; an Emerald Brooch every 16 days | an Illuminated Book every 16 days; a Gold Circlet every 32 days | none yet (kept for M29's Legends and M33's offices) |

What each class gives besides its tax (34.7):

| Class | Perk |
|---|---|
| Artisan | crafters who are Artisans or better (carpenter, mason, tinkerer, chef, dyer, toolsmith, vintner, tailor, printer, jeweller) work 10% faster, inside the shared 2× pace cap (`maxWorkPace`, 30.2) |
| Burgher | Burgher scholars research 15% faster; with 3 Burgher households the village may send one more caravan route, and market day brings a fourth trader selling a grand-house blueprint (Townhouse or Manor) for 12 emeralds |
| Noble | each Noble household lifts the village's wellbeing 3% (9% at most); a village with a Noble holds a **Noble's Ball** in place of every other festival: guests gather at the Manor's ballroom (else the hall), wine and the store's best food are served, gold fireworks at dusk, +15 mood for 3 days for everyone who came, and players there are Heroes of the Village for the night |
| Legends (M29) | live among the Nobles: held to the Noble's needs and counted with them (their home need is M29's "tier III house"); does nothing until M29's Legend engine finds Legends |

Drinks: Cider 2 hunger, Berry Wine 3, Vintage Wine 4 and 5 s of Regeneration; the bottle comes back. Players buy today's
Gazette for 1 emerald and an Illuminated Book for 8; every trade buys its makings and sells its goods at every level.
The Gazette is printed from the hall that day (the newest chronicle entries, the open quests and rewards, the next
festival and market day, the week's births, weddings and households that rose); the Illuminated Book is the village's
whole chronicle bound in gold. A new hall quest, **Spread the news**, asks a player to carry this week's Gazette to a
village on one of this village's caravan routes, paid like a delivery.

Pokémon that help (pastured, `Partners`): Vintner Grass, Bug, Fairy; Tailor Bug, Normal; Printer Psychic, Normal;
Jeweller Rock, Steel, Fairy.

Outfits (villager and zombie villager, pixel-art skill): Vintner a wine-stained apron, rolled sleeves, a straw hat
with a vine band; Tailor a tape measure round the neck, a pincushion at the wrist, a neat waistcoat; Printer an
ink-stained apron, a green visor, sleeve garters; Jeweller a loupe over one eye (the `glasses` helper), a dark velvet
waistcoat with gold buttons.

Workshops (34.13, 34.14; each with a II, grows in villages in all five styles with its worker, weight 2, sold by its
Journeymen): the **Winery** (press house over a half-sunk cellar; II a terraced berry garden), the **Tailor's Shop**
(timber shop with a bay window; II a cutting-room storey), the **Print Shop** (brick and timber, a skylight over the
press; II a bindery and reading room), the **Jeweller's Workshop** (small stone shop, an iron door; II a strong room).

### The new jobs' blocks and items (vetoable, like the 21.1a plan)

No new job blocks: each new job shares a vanilla workstation and is picked by sneak-right-clicking a villager standing
by it with an item, exactly as the 21.1a shared stations work (`work/Stations`). A jobless villager who walks up to the
block by themselves still takes the block's vanilla job.

| New job | Block | Item that picks it | Who else works there (their item) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vintner (`aliveworkplace:vintner`) | cauldron (its vat: purple splashes and a squelch while pressing) | sweet berries, glow berries or an apple | Leatherworker / dyer (leather), Sifter (gravel, sand, red sand, soul sand) |
| Tailor (`aliveworkplace:tailor`) | loom (becomes a shared station) | string | Shepherd (now picked with shears) |
| Printer (`aliveworkplace:printer`) | cartography table | ink sac | Cartographer (compass), Netherworker (netherrack) |
| Jeweller (`aliveworkplace:jeweller`) | stonecutter | **a gold nugget** (proposed; the roadmap says an amethyst shard, see below) | Mason (clay ball), Gem Grower (amethyst shard) |

**A clash found while writing this:** the roadmap gives the Jeweller the amethyst shard, but since 28.11 an amethyst
shard at a stonecutter already picks the **Gem Grower** (`GemGrowers.isShard`), and the Mason is picked with a clay
ball (not cobblestone, as 34.12 says). Default until the owner says otherwise: the **Jeweller is picked with a gold
nugget** (a jeweller's metal, used in two of their three goods, and nothing else at the stonecutter takes it); the
Mason keeps the clay ball and the Gem Grower the shard. 34.12's test "picked with an amethyst shard" becomes "picked with
a gold nugget".

A home's grade is its blueprint's tier (`Homes`: a base blueprint is I, its `_2` upgrade II, `_3` III), unless a
`homes/` file gives it more; each upgrade adds one, up to III.

| Blueprint | Grade | Upgrades | Sold by builders |
|---|---|---|---|
| Any house a builder built today | its tier (I to III) | as today | as today |
| Artisan's House (a narrow timber house, workroom below, two beds above) | I | II: a back workshop wing and a third bed (grade II) | from Apprentice |
| Burgher's Townhouse (three storeys of brick and timber, a shop window, two bedrooms) | II | II: a walled courtyard garden and a third bedroom (grade III); III: a corner tower with a study (grade III, the cap) | once the village has an Artisan household |
| Noble's Manor (two storeys of stone round a hall with a grand stair, a dining room, two bedrooms, slate roof with dormers) | III | II: a walled formal garden, hedges, a fountain, gravel walks (counts 3 beauty for its people); III: a gatehouse and a ballroom wing (where the ball is held) | once the village has a Burgher household |

### Elders, retirement and family trees (34.19 to 34.21)

| What | Number |
|---|---|
| Grown days before a villager is an elder (`villagerElderDays`) | 120 |
| Elder's walk | 15% slower |
| Elder's mood, when fed and housed ("a quiet old age") | +5 |
| Elder chatter lines | 4 |
| Retirements a village | at most 1 every 3 days |
| Days the successor shadows the elder ("learning from Bram") | 2 |
| Successor's level | one below the elder's, at least Apprentice |
| A mentored Novice or Apprentice learns | 50% faster ("taught by Bram") |
| `elderPassing` (owner's call, off) | if on: an elder passes in their sleep after 40 elder days and leaves a grave |
| Book of Families | up to 400 people, the longest dead forgotten first |
| Family names | a list of 60 (`family_name.aliveworkplace.<n>`), by UUID |
| A family's tree printed by the Printer | 2 emeralds |

A successor is the elder's own grown child without a job, else any jobless grown villager of a class the job allows.
An elder never retires while they hold something for a player or a build: a builder's site or queue, a miner's quarry,
Pokémon in their daycare, fossils being revived, parcels on their round. The successor gets the job's assignments
(field, tree farm, orchard, courier routes, patrol route, ball orders, who hired them). A retired elder never takes a
job again (players can still trade with them); by day they stand by a Novice or Apprentice of their old trade, else sit
by the hall or on a bench.

### The first week after the update (34.22)

The moment the owner's server runs 1.8, every village with a hall switches on by itself:

1. **The first minutes:** each hall sorts its people, 16 a round (a round is every 30 seconds, so a City of 36 takes
   about a minute and a half). Each household gets the **highest class it already qualifies for, not counting
   luxuries, and at most Burgher** (Nobles are earned). Everyone gets an age spread over the last 100 days and a family
   name. One chronicle entry and one chat line: "The people of Thornholm found their places: 14 peasants, 6 artisans,
   2 burghers".
2. **Days 1 to 7, "Settling in":** nobody falls a class, luxuries are asked for but not yet needed, nobody retires. The
   hall shows "Settling in: 5 days left" and what each class will need, so there's a week to set up Vintners,
   Tailors, Printers and Jewellers.
3. **From day 8:** the ladder runs for real. Households missing their class's luxuries for 3 dawns come down a step;
   those who have them rise. The first elders appear about three weeks in (the oldest seeded ages reach 120 days about
   21 days after the update), one at a time.
4. **What never changes:** nobody loses a job (gates only apply to new hires), ranks keep their thresholds, the treasury
   never takes in less than before, and build sites, quarries, daycare Pokémon, fossils, parcels and every chest are
   untouched. Turning `villageClasses` off brings back today's behaviour with nothing lost.

Back up the world before updating (34.23 writes the owner's step-by-step guide, `docs/owner/updating-to-1.8.md`).

## Technical detail

### Where it lives

New core classes: `people/SocialClasses` (the loader of `classes/` and the dawn check), `people/ClassNeeds` (the need
toolbox), `people/Households`, `hall/Services` (loader of `services/` and the hall's daily list), `people/Luxuries`
(loader of `luxuries/` and the dawn taking), `craft/LuxuryWork` and `craft/LuxuryRecipes` (loader of `luxury_recipes/`,
planned through a new `Crafting.Kind.LUXURY`), `people/HomeGrades` (loader of `homes/`, read by `Homes`),
`people/LifeStages`, `people/Retirement`, `people/FamilyBook`, `hall/ClassMigration`. Client: the class and elder render
layers and the small class/stage packet (34.18), sent through a new `Platform` event when a player starts tracking a
villager. All names are proposals; the items may rename them.

Everything runs in the hall's round (`VillageHallBlockEntity`, every `VillageNeeds.CHECK_EVERY` = 600 ticks), never on a
per-tick or area scan: the dawn class check takes **8 households a round**, seeding (34.22) **16 villagers a round**.
Classes exist only in villages with a hall, like moods; outside one nobody has a class and nothing is gated.

### Data formats

All under `data/aliveworkplace/` (or a data pack's own namespace), one file per entry, loaded by reload listeners
(`Platform.get().onDataReload`, like `edicts/` and `guilds/`). As there: a malformed file is logged with its name and
field and skipped; `"enabled": false` at the same path switches one of ours off; texts are text components
(`{"translate": "..."}` in ours, a plain string allowed in a pack's); item, job (profession) and blueprint ids are
registry ids; an item field also takes a `#tag`; files may carry `"fabric:load_conditions"` (checked through
`Platform`, as the Trainers' Guild file is).

**`classes/<id>.json`** (34.2; ours: `peasant`, `artisan`, `burgher`, `noble`):

```json
{
  "tier": 1,
  "name": {"translate": "class.aliveworkplace.artisan"},
  "tax": 1.5,
  "outfit": "aliveworkplace:textures/entity/villager/class/artisan.png",
  "needs": [
    {"type": "home", "grade": 1},
    {"type": "fed_days", "days": 3},
    {"type": "diet", "kind": "varied"},
    {"type": "services", "count": 1, "any": ["chapel", "school", "clinic"]},
    {"type": "luxury", "id": "aliveworkplace:work_clothes"}
  ],
  "wants": [
    {"type": "luxury", "id": "aliveworkplace:cider"},
    {"type": "services", "count": 1, "any": ["tavern"]}
  ],
  "jobs": [
    "aliveworkplace:tinkerer", "aliveworkplace:chef", "aliveworkplace:netherworker", "aliveworkplace:nurse",
    "aliveworkplace:teacher", "aliveworkplace:shopkeeper", "aliveworkplace:innkeeper", "aliveworkplace:printer",
    "aliveworkplace:ball_smith", "aliveworkplace:tutor", "aliveworkplace:pokemon_trader", "aliveworkplace:fossil_scientist"
  ],
  "effects": [
    {"type": "aliveworkplace:work_pace", "percent": 10, "jobs": [
      "aliveworkplace:carpenter", "minecraft:mason", "aliveworkplace:tinkerer", "aliveworkplace:chef",
      "minecraft:leatherworker", "minecraft:toolsmith", "aliveworkplace:vintner", "aliveworkplace:tailor",
      "aliveworkplace:printer", "aliveworkplace:jeweller"]}
  ],
  "enabled": true
}
```

- `tier` 0 to 3 orders the ladder (one file per tier; a pack replaces ours by path). `tax` is the factor on
  `treasuryPerWorker`. `outfit` is the class layer's texture (absent: no layer, as for `peasant`).
- `jobs`: a job id the client doesn't have (the Cobblemon jobs without Cobblemon) is ignored, so one file serves both.
  Vanilla professions in `jobs` are ignored with a warning: vanilla jobs are never gated (owner's call).
- `effects` use M30's typed effect toolbox (`hall/CivicEffects`) with its optional `jobs` filter, applied to the
  class's members (and, for `work_pace`, to every class above, since perks say "or better"). New types for this
  milestone: `research_pace` (`percent`, Burghers' 15%), `caravan_routes` (`extra`, `households` 3), `market_traders`
  (M30's, with `households` 3 and a `sells` blueprint list at 12 emeralds), `wellbeing` (`percent` 3 per household,
  `max` 9) and `festival` (`replace_every` 2, `kind` `aliveworkplace:noble_ball`). A household-count condition
  (`households`) on an effect means "once the village has that many households of this class or higher".

The need toolbox (`people/ClassNeeds`; `"type"` picks the codec, unknown types are logged and count as unmet):

| Type | Fields | Holds when | Reads |
|---|---|---|---|
| `home` | `grade` | the building their bed is in is at least that grade | `Homes.of` + `homes/` |
| `fed_days` | `days` | they ate from the store on each of the last `days` days | `LAST_MEAL`, plus a fed-days count in `class_progress` |
| `diet` | `kind` (`varied`, `plain`) | `Diet.of` is that kind or better | `Diet` |
| `beauty` | `points` | the decorations finished within 16 blocks of home are worth that many points | `Decorations.points`, as `Moods` |
| `building` | `blueprint`, optional `tier` | a finished build of it (or a styled or upgraded one) stands in the village | `BuildSiteManager.finishedNear`, `BlueprintStyles.base` |
| `village_rank` | `rank` (`hamlet`, `village`, `town`, `city`) | the village is that rank or higher | `VillageRanks` |
| `services` | `count` and `any`, or `all` | at least `count` of the `any` services, or every `all` one, reach their home | the hall's service list (34.3) |
| `luxury` | `id` | they had one less than its `every_days` ago | `luxuries_had` (34.4); never holds before 34.4 |

How the starting ladder is written with them: Artisan as above. **Burgher**: `home` 2; `fed_days` 3; `diet` varied;
`services` `all: [school]`; `services` `count: 2, any: [school, chapel, clinic, library, tavern]`; `building`
`aliveworkplace:market_square`; `luxury` fine_clothes, berry_wine, gazette; wants `luxury` amethyst_ring, `services`
`all: [library]`, `beauty` 2. **Noble**: `home` 3; `fed_days` 3; `diet` varied; `services` `all: [chapel, school,
clinic, library]`; `village_rank` town; `beauty` 3; `luxury` noble_robes, vintage_wine, emerald_brooch; wants `luxury`
illuminated_book, gold_circlet. **Peasant**: `tier` 0, `tax` 1, no needs, wants, jobs or effects.

**`services/<id>.json`** (34.3; ours: `chapel`, `school`, `clinic`, `library`, `market`, `tavern`):

```json
{
  "name": {"translate": "service.aliveworkplace.school"},
  "jobs": ["aliveworkplace:teacher"],
  "blueprints": ["aliveworkplace:schoolhouse"],
  "range": 48,
  "icon": "minecraft:lectern",
  "enabled": true
}
```

| Service | Workers (by their job site) | Finished builds | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| chapel | none | Chapel (`aliveworkplace:chapel`) | 48 |
| school | Teacher | Schoolhouse (`aliveworkplace:schoolhouse`) | 48 |
| clinic | Nurse | Healing Center (`aliveworkplace:healing_center`) | 48 |
| library | Scholar, Librarian (`minecraft:librarian`) | Library (`aliveworkplace:library`) | 48 |
| market | none | Market Square (`aliveworkplace:market_square`) | village-wide (`"range": -1`) |
| tavern | Innkeeper | Inn (`aliveworkplace:inn`) | 48 |

Styled builds and upgrades count as their base (`BlueprintStyles.base`, `BlueprintUpgrades`). The hall works the list
out once a day (workers by their job site, builds from `BuildSiteManager.finishedNear` over the hall's reach) and keeps
it; a worker who quits stops counting at the next dawn.

**`luxuries/<id>.json`** (34.4 ships the engine; the eleven files `cider`, `berry_wine`, `vintage_wine`,
`work_clothes`, `fine_clothes`, `noble_robes`, `gazette`, `illuminated_book`, `amethyst_ring`, `emerald_brooch`,
`gold_circlet` land with their jobs, 34.9 to 34.12):

```json
{
  "item": "aliveworkplace:berry_wine",
  "every_days": 2,
  "enabled": true
}
```

`item` is an item or `#tag`. A luxury is **due** when the household's `luxuries_had` day for it is at least
`every_days` ago (or absent). At dawn, in 34.2's slices, each household takes one of every due luxury of its own class
and of the class above (by the `luxury` needs and wants in those class files) from `VillageNeeds.store` (the kitchens'
chests, then the Storehouses'), and the day is noted; a couple takes one between them. Taking comes before checking, so
a household that gets its Berry Wine at dawn has the need that day; an empty store leaves the need unmet that day.

**`luxury_recipes/<id>.json`** (34.5; ours land with each job: `cider`, `berry_wine`, `berry_wine_glow`,
`berry_wine_cobblemon` (Cobblemon load condition, `#cobblemon:berries`), `vintage_wine`, `work_clothes`,
`fine_clothes`, `noble_robes_purple`/`_blue`/`_red`/`_black`, `books`, `gazette`, `illuminated_book`, `amethyst_ring`,
`emerald_brooch`, `gold_circlet`):

```json
{
  "job": "aliveworkplace:vintner",
  "level": 3,
  "inputs": [{"item": "aliveworkplace:berry_wine", "count": 1, "min_age_days": 3}],
  "output": {"id": "aliveworkplace:vintage_wine", "count": 1},
  "ticks": 200,
  "enabled": true
}
```

- `level`: the lowest job level that may make it (1 Novice, 2 Apprentice, 3 Journeyman). `inputs[].item` is an item
  or `#tag`; `min_age_days` (optional) needs an input whose `made_day` is that many days back. `output.count` defaults
  to 1 (books and the Gazette are 2).
- One recipe per way of making a good: "6 sweet berries or 4 glow berries" is two files; "5 wool of one rich colour" is
  four files, so the engine needs no "same colour" rule. "Not white" is a tag, `#aliveworkplace:dyed_wool` (the 15
  coloured wools).
- `LuxuryWork` (a `CrafterWork`, like `ChefWork`) makes, in turn, whatever the store holds fewer than 8 of, from its own
  chests, the store and the village's stashes, stock orders first (`StockOrders`); vanilla makings may be planned up to
  the usual two steps down; missing makings go on the requests board; Craftsmanship research speeds it.
- The Gazette and the Illuminated Book are written when made (`written_book_content` filled from the hall that day);
  `ticks` is before `Pace`.

**`homes/<name>.json`** (34.15; ours: `artisans_house`, `townhouse`, `manor`):

```json
{
  "blueprint": "aliveworkplace:townhouse",
  "grade": 2,
  "enabled": true
}
```

`grade` (1 to 3) is the base blueprint's grade; each upgrade (`_2`, `_3`) adds one, up to III; styled versions count
as their base. A blueprint with no file keeps its tier as its grade, so every house built today keeps its grade.

### Config switches (`config/aliveworkplace.json`, all in the Mod Menu screen)

Every switch below belongs to M34 in `Expansions` (a new `M34` flag, `false` until the whole milestone is done, B76):
until then they stay off and hidden whatever the file says, and GameTests open the gate. A missing value takes its
default; no `configVersion` bump.

| Switch | Default | Range | Off means |
|---|---|---|---|
| `villageClasses` | true (off in GameTests unless a test turns it on) | | no classes, needs, luxuries taken, class perks or job gates: today's behaviour; classes, progress and `luxuries_had` stay saved and come back when it's on |
| `classRiseDays` | 2 | 1 to 30 | dawns running the next class's needs must hold to rise |
| `classFallDays` | 3 | 1 to 30 | dawns running a need of their own class must fail to fall |
| `classGraceDays` | 7 | 0 to 30 | the settling-in days after a hall is first seeded (34.22) |
| `classOutfits` | true | | no class or elder layers drawn (the client's own config decides on the client) |
| `villagerAges` | true | | nobody becomes an elder (no look, slow walk, mood or chatter); `adult_since` is still kept |
| `villagerElderDays` | 120 | 20 to 1000 | grown days before an elder |
| `elderPassing` | **false** (owner's call) | | elders never die of old age; on: an elder passes in their sleep after 40 elder days and leaves a grave |
| `elderRetirement` | true | | elders keep working; nobody shadows or mentors |
| `familyNames` | true | | no family names on the hall or in the chronicle; the Book of Families and the Family button still work by first names |

The existing `treasuryPerWorker` (20) and `maxWorkPace` (200) are read, not changed.

Not saved: where the dawn slices have got to (34.2 uses each household's `class_progress.day` instead of a hall `classCursor`: a household is counted once a day, in a fixed order by UUID, so a reload part-way through a dawn loses nothing), households (worked out from `Couples.Partner` and beds each dawn), whether someone is an elder (from
`adult_since`), mentoring (where a retired elder stands that day), the class perks' sums (rebuilt on load, as M30's).
A villager's `elder` and class reach the client by the 34.18 packet only.

### As built in 34.2

- `people/SocialClasses` (loader and dawn check), `people/ClassNeeds` (the toolbox), `people/Households`. The hall's
  round calls `SocialClasses.round`. A day's first round finds the village's people (one entity lookup, no block
  scans) and reads the village (its finished builds and their boxes) for the day; the next rounds count 8 households
  each down that list, then nothing more until the next dawn. Timed with 60 households: about 0.6 ms of the server
  thread a round on average, the slowest round under 2 ms (`ClassGameTests`).
- `services` reads `ClassNeeds.services` and `luxury` reads `ClassNeeds.luxuryEvery` with `luxuries_had`: hooks that
  34.3 (the hall's service list) and 34.4 (the luxury files) fill. Until then no service reaches anyone and no luxury
  need holds. `luxuries_had` is declared now (34.2), since the `luxury` need reads it.
- `home` reads `Homes` (a blueprint's tier) until 34.15's `homes/` files.
- `effects` are kept as written until 34.7 reads them (its new effect types don't exist yet).
- A married couple whose two classes differ lives as the higher one and is held to its needs; both get its class.
- A child whose bed is in no builder's building (or one where nobody grown has a class) keeps the class they have.
  Children carry a `class_progress` too, only for its `day` (counted today).
- Villagers without `social_class` are left alone: seeding them is 34.22's.

### As built in 34.3

- `hall/Services` loads `services/` (a data pack replaces one by path, adds one, or switches one off with
  `"enabled": false`; a file with neither `jobs` nor `blueprints` is skipped with a warning; `range` -1 is village-wide;
  `icon` defaults to `minecraft:bell`) and sets `ClassNeeds.services` when the mod starts.
- The hall's round works the list out once a day from the census's workers (a villager whose job is in `jobs` counts at
  their `JOB_SITE`) and `BuildSiteManager.finishedNear(hall, VillageHalls.RADIUS)` (a build counts at its origin; its
  style and upgrades are stripped with `ClassNeeds.root`, which is now public). Only while `villageClasses` is on; the
  `services` need also works it out on first ask if the round hasn't that day. Each home is then checked against the
  kept list (distance from their bed), with no entity or block lookups per household. Saved as hall NBT `services` and
  `servicesDay` (absent in older halls: empty, 0).
- Every named worker and build exists. Two more builds count, since they are the same service as workplaces: the
  School workplace (`aliveworkplace:school`) for school and the Clinic workplace (`aliveworkplace:clinic`) for clinic.
  The chapel and the market have no worker, as the table says, so their "found by its worker" is that they have none.
- Names: `service.aliveworkplace.<id>` in `en_us.json`.

### The migration (34.22), step by step

When a hall's round finds `classesVersion` 0 and `villageClasses` on:

1. **Seed 16 villagers this round** (the next 16 in the village without `social_class`; mercenaries (`mercenary_until`)
   and inn guests (`traveller`) skipped). For each grown one:
   - work out their household (partner by `Couples.partner`, both seeded together);
   - give it the highest class from Burgher down whose needs **other than `luxury`** hold now (never Noble); write
     `social_class` on both and a fresh `class_progress`;
   - `adult_since` = today − (a number 0 to 99 from their UUID), so ages are spread over the last 100 days and the first
     elder comes 21 days after seeding at the earliest (120 − 99), one at a time;
   - `family_name` from their UUID unless a parent's is known (children then take it).
   Children are seeded with their household's class after the grown-ups of their building.
2. **Next round, the next 16**, until nobody is left unseeded. A crash or a save part-way loses nothing: the next round
   re-seeds only those still without `social_class`, and a villager already seeded is never re-seeded.
3. **When the last one is seeded:** `classesVersion` = 1, `classGraceUntil` = today + `classGraceDays`; one `CLASS`
   chronicle entry with the counts ("The people of Thornholm found their places: 14 peasants, 6 artisans, 2
   burghers") and one chat line to the players in the village.
4. **The grace (7 days):** nobody falls (the `missed` count isn't kept), luxuries are taken when due but don't count as
   needed (rising still needs them), nobody retires; the hall shows "Settling in: N days left" and each class's needs.
5. **What is never touched:** jobs (gates apply only when a job is taken, so nobody is fired), rank thresholds, the
   treasury (a Peasant pays today's rate, higher classes more, so takings never drop), build sites, quarries, daycare
   Pokémon, fossils, parcels and container contents.
6. **Later and odd cases:** a hall first loaded weeks after the update goes through the same steps with its own grace;
   a villager who joins later is seeded at the next round (Peasant unless they qualify; a hired traveller by level:
   Apprentice Peasant, Journeyman Artisan, Expert Burgher); `villageClasses` off skips everything and keeps the fields;
   `/workplace classes <status|reseed>` (operators, the nearest hall) prints the counts and grace, or sets
   `classesVersion` 0 and clears that hall's villagers' `social_class` and `class_progress` to seed again.

34.22's test village (a save from before 1.8: a Scholar in a plain house, a married couple, a child, a builder
mid-build, a rancher with daycare boarders, a City-rank hall) proves each step, including a reload part-way through
seeding and a second round that changes nothing. 34.23 rehearses it on a real Cobbleverse world (a Hamlet of 6, a Town
of 22, a City of 36) for 2 in-game days.

### Other decisions taken (not the owner's)

- **The Jeweller's item is a gold nugget, not an amethyst shard** (the shard already picks the Gem Grower, 28.11), and
  the Mason stays on the clay ball (34.12 says cobblestone). The Shepherd is picked with shears once the loom is shared.
- **Each class file lists all of its needs**; nothing is inherited from the class below. The Noble's file repeats the
  Burgher's food and diet (fed 3 days, a varied diet), which the roadmap's Noble line leaves out; without it a Noble
  could live on bread alone.
- **Burgher's services:** "a school and one more service" is two `services` needs (school; and any two of school,
  chapel, clinic, library, tavern), and the Market Square is a `building` need, so the market doesn't double as the
  "one more".
- **A luxury need holds** while the last one was had less than `every_days` ago; taking at dawn comes before the
  check.
- **The Gazette and the Illuminated Book are our own items** that read like a written book (vanilla's
  `written_book_content` on them), so a luxury file can name them and any old written book doesn't count. The family
  tree the Printer prints is a plain written book.
- **Taxes** (34.7): each worker's share is `treasuryPerWorker × class tax × (1 + 0.1 × wants had)`, then today's
  wellbeing and rank factors; a jobless Noble counts as a worker. The treasury's cap is unchanged.
- **Chronicle kinds:** `CLASS` (34.6) and one more, `LIFE`, for elders, retirements and family generations, rather
  than a kind each.
- **Config ranges** above are this note's; the roadmap gives only the defaults.
- **Hall round cadence:** 600 ticks, so the 8-household dawn slices finish a City's check within a few minutes of dawn
  and seeding 16 a round takes a 100-villager City under 4 minutes.

## For the owner to decide

Defaults hold until you say otherwise; lanes build with them.

1. **Elders never die of old age** (`elderPassing` off). If you'd like it on: an elder passes in their sleep after 40
   elder days (160 days after growing up) and leaves a grave; the family tree marks them.
2. **Vanilla jobs are never class-gated**: Farmers, Librarians, Masons and the rest stay keyed on their blocks, as in
   vanilla; only our own jobs need a class.
3. **Family names show on the hall but never rename a villager**: "Dara (Ashby)" in the hall's lists and the chronicle;
   the name over their head (or from a Name Tag) stays as it is.
4. **The Jeweller is picked with a gold nugget at the stonecutter** (the amethyst shard is already the Gem Grower's).
   Say if you'd rather another item.
5. **The class outfits**: pick one of three for each class and for the elder look on the picker page (34.17); until
   then the first of each is used.
