The Village Hall turns a group of villagers into a village that lives: everyone gets a name, the village has needs, a
rank, a treasury and a chronicle, and you can see all of it in one screen.

## Putting one down

Craft a **Village Hall** (gold, a book and gold over planks, with an emerald in the middle) and put it in the middle of
your village; by the bell is a good spot. Everyone within 64 blocks belongs to it. The village gets a made-up name
(*Thornholm*, *Ashford*...); use a **Name Tag** on the hall to call it what you like (the tag isn't used up).

Or have your builder build one: the [Village Hall build](village-hall-build.md) is a timber hall with the Village Hall
block inside, a bell under its porch and a notice board, and it works just like a hall you put down yourself.

**Moving it.** Mine the hall with an axe and it drops itself, keeping a name you gave it. While it's gone, the village
leaves the caravans' trade routes and its open quests are dropped, so put it back soon.

## The hall's screen

Right-click the hall. It looks like the Blueprint Table's screen, with a **sidebar** down the left: the village's
name and today's date ("Spring, day 3 · Year 1") at the top, then ten buttons, each with a picture and a word:

| Button | What's in it |
|---|---|
| **Overview** | the village at a glance (name and rank, villagers, beds, food, guards, wellbeing, what workers wait for, buildings going up, quests) and "What next?" |
| **People** | everyone who lives there, their wellbeing and what they wait for, the bell that calls everyone home, Legends, classes and free job blocks |
| **Homes & Food** | beds and food in store |
| **Buildings** | buildings going up and their looks, repairs, upgrades, Wonders and the Steward's desk |
| **Defense** | guards and the Defense page, the bell, mercenaries |
| **Quests** | the village's quests |
| **Trade** | caravans and trade routes (prices, pacts and colonies when they're on), the bank |
| **Calendar** | the calendar, festivals, the Festival Cup and the chronicle |
| **Laws & Realm** | the treasury and protection (the name icon), the Book of Edicts and your realm |
| **Map** | draw a map of the village |

Click a button and its things fill the right side as a list, each with its picture, its name and a line about it. A
section with nothing in it says so in plain words ("No guards yet."), and its buttons stay there for you to use. The
hall remembers the section you opened last until you leave the game. With nothing under the mouse, the pane on the
right shows what the village needs next on the Overview, and what a section is for on the others. Walk more than 8
blocks away from the hall (or break it) and the screen closes. Friends can have the hall open at the same time: each
of you gets your own screen and keeps your own section.

**People is a table** of everyone who lives in the village: their name, job, level and mood, with the count on top
("8 · 6 working · 2 jobless"). Scroll it with the mouse wheel in a big village. The mood is one word: Happy, Content,
Unhappy, or **No bed** in red when having no bed is what's wrong. Children and builders are in the list too ("Child"
in the Job column, builders in gold). Click a villager's row to open their details right under it: their traits,
what they're doing now, what they need, their mood and why, and their class and how to rise. Under that are the
buttons: **Job ▾** changes their job (or gives a jobless villager one), **Bed ▾** shows the village's beds,
**Find** makes them glow so you can spot them, and **Story** (for a villager with a name) opens their life story:
your hearts with them, their name day, their family and partner, and what they've told you. A long row can be
scrolled through: its details and lists stay in view while you scroll down to them. Click the row again (or press
Esc) to close it. The bell, the figures
and the Legends, Classes and Free job blocks pages sit in a row of pictures over the table.

**Giving a bed.** Bed ▾ opens the village's beds under the villager's row. Free beds come first, with the house and
which bed when a builder put it up, or how far and which way from the hall ("free · 14 blocks east"). Then the beds
others sleep in: click one to swap ("swap with Jem"; you need a bed to give in return). Click a free bed and it's
theirs at once, their old bed freed, and they sleep there tonight. A broken bed houses nobody. No free beds? "Short of
beds? Plan a house ›" opens Buildings. Wagon beds and cradles aren't listed.

**Changing a job.** Job ▾ opens a list of every job under the villager's row. On top is what it costs, by their
level (5 emeralds for a Novice up to 50 for a Master), and what they lose: their trades and level, so they start again
as a Novice. Jobs with a free work block in the village come first and say how many ("Mason · 1 free Stonecutter");
the others are gray and say which block to place first ("Mason · place a Stonecutter first"). Click a job once to see
the price, and again to pay: the job and the block are theirs at once, and they walk over. A builder who was building
hands the build back, and its blueprint comes back to you, still placed, for another builder. **No job** at the bottom
is free: click it twice and they stop working. A villager with no job is given a free block for nothing, in one click.
Children and nitwits can't change jobs, and with the game rule `workplaceRetraining` off the button isn't there.

**Point at anything and the pane on the right tells you all about it.** Move the mouse on into the pane and it keeps
reading the same thing, so you can scroll a long entry there with the mouse wheel without clicking it (or hold Shift
and scroll over the row); a little arrow at the bottom shows there's more. A page you open from a section (the
chronicle, the Book of Edicts...) fills the right side; click a sidebar button to go back to a list.

![The hall's sidebar](https://jcondedata.github.io/minecraft-alive-workplace/hall_sidebar/still-1.jpg)

Each worker is shown as their workstation, stacked as high as their level, with what they're doing ("Market Stall ·
45%") and what they're waiting for. **Click a villager to make them glow** for ten seconds so you can find them; they stay in the pane, where the mouse
wheel scrolls through everything about them (the × or Esc puts them away). Click a
villager without a job to see the free workstations, and click one to give them that job.

**Changing a villager's job.** Right-click a villager who already works and their page opens, with a **Change job** button
(the emerald). It lists every free job block in the village, plus the Builder (who needs a free Blueprint Table: with
none free, the Builder's entry tells you to place one). Click a job to see the price, then click it again to pay: 5
emeralds for a Novice up to 50 for a Master, the same as [retraining at a Blueprint Table](jobs.md). The new job is
theirs the moment you pay, and the hall's list shows it straight away. They walk to their new block, and if they
can't get there within about a minute (walled in, too far, stuck) they're moved right beside it. Their old block stays
where it is, free for someone else, and a builder hands back their build first. Their old job's trades and level are
lost. Children and nitwits can't change jobs, and with the game rule `workplaceRetraining` off the button isn't there.

![Changing a villager's job from the hall](https://jcondedata.github.io/minecraft-alive-workplace/hall_retrain/still-2.jpg)

**Filling empty job blocks.** The **Free job blocks** tab (the crafting table) lists every job block in your village
that nobody works at, nearest the hall first: the job it gives, the building it's in (say, "In the Storehouse") and how
far away it is, with how many villagers without a job could take it. Click a block to see those villagers, then click
one: they take the job and walk over to their new block. It's the easy way to fill a lectern or a Storehouse inside a
finished house, with no need to lead anyone indoors. Several blocks of one kind in one building share a line with a
count ("Fisherman ×4"). A block that can give several jobs says how many ("Composter: 4 jobs") and lists them: click it,
pick the job you want (Farmer, Orchard Keeper, Florist or Composter), then who takes it. If someone else got the block
first, you're told and nothing changes. Villagers who already have a job aren't on the list: to give one of them a new job, use **Change job** on their
page (above) for a few emeralds.

![The Free job blocks page](https://jcondedata.github.io/minecraft-alive-workplace/hall_jobs/still-1.jpg)

**Upgrading a home.** The **Upgrades** tab (the bricks) lists every finished build in your village that has a next
level, like a Starter Cottage that can become a Starter Cottage II, nearest the hall first. Each one shows its level and
the next, where it is, what the next level costs in blocks, and whether there's room for it. Click a build with room and
your Builder goes and builds the next level right over the old one. Grass, dirt, stone, trees, snow or water in the
way don't matter: the Builder clears them as part of the job. A wall, a fence, a path, a chest or another building does,
and the page tells you what's in the way and its exact spot, so you can move it and try again. The villagers who live
in the house keep their beds the whole time.

![The Upgrades page](https://jcondedata.github.io/minecraft-alive-workplace/hall_upgrade/still-1.jpg)

![The Village Hall's screen](https://jcondedata.github.io/minecraft-alive-workplace/hall/still-4.jpg)

The **bell** in the middle calls everyone home: villagers who wandered off are brought back beside the hall.

## The hall's pages

- **Quests** (the map icon): the village's quests for you. See [Quests](quests.md).
- **The chronicle** (the book): what happened in the village, newest first: babies born, buildings finished and who
  built them, raids, weddings, quests done.
- **Trade routes** (the minecart): caravans to other villages. See [Trade and caravans](trade-and-caravans.md).
- **What next?** (the compass): what the village lacks most, from a builder or beds to guards or decorations, each with
  how to put it right.
- **[The village map](map-of.md)**: draws the village on an empty map from your inventory, every building marked and colored by what
  it is.
- **The calendar**: the season, the day, and when the next festival is.
- **Mercenaries** (the iron sword): hire three fighters for a night. See [Threats and defense](defence.md).
- **Free job blocks** (the crafting table tab): the job blocks nobody works at, and who could take each.
- **Upgrades** (the bricks tab): your finished homes and workshops that can go up a level, and a Builder to send.

![The chronicle](https://jcondedata.github.io/minecraft-alive-workplace/hall_pages/still-1.jpg)

## Ranks

A village grows from a **Hamlet** to a **Village** (10 villagers and 5 finished buildings), a **Town** (20 villagers,
12 buildings and 3 levels of research) and a **City** (35, 25 and 7). Every rank pays: quests pay a quarter more,
caravans can go to one more village, more traders come on market day, and the village can grow ten villagers bigger. A
rank up is celebrated with fireworks over the hall. Hover over the hall's name icon to see what the next rank still
needs.

## The treasury

Every morning the village puts by its takings: about a fifth of an emerald a worker, more in a well-kept village and a
quarter more for each rank. The hall's name icon (top left) shows what's there; click it to collect. It holds up to a
stack of emeralds per rank, so don't leave it too long.

![The treasury and protection](https://jcondedata.github.io/minecraft-alive-workplace/hall_treasury/still-1.jpg)

## Protecting your village

Whoever places the hall owns it. **Shift-click the hall's name icon** to protect the village: inside its area only you,
your friends (`/workplace friend add <name>`) and operators can break or place blocks, open chests, or hurt the
villagers and animals. Anyone can still walk in, open doors, ring the bell and trade. Shift-click again to open it up.
It's off until you turn it on.

## The Village Ledger

Craft a **[Village Ledger](village-ledger.md)** (a book and an emerald) and right-click your hall with it. From then on, right-click the air
anywhere nearby to open the hall's screen without walking back. It stays open however far you are, as long as the
hall stands.

## Needs

A village with a hall has needs: food in store, a bed for everyone, a guard for every ten villagers, light by the beds,
and decorations. How well they're met is the village's **wellbeing**, and it sets the pace of all the work: up to a
quarter faster when everyone's fed, housed and safe, and slower when they're hungry and sleeping rough. More in
[Village life](village-life.md).
