The better classes live on more than bread. Wine, clothes, the news and jewelry are **luxuries**: four workers make
them, the village store keeps them, and every [household](households.md) of the right class takes one at dawn when its
last one has worn out.

A **need** is part of what a class is: a household that goes three dawns without one comes down a class, and one that
lacks a need of the next class can't rise. A **want** is a bonus: each want a household has makes it pay 10% more tax.

## How they move

1. The maker keeps the store in 8 of each thing they know, from the makings in the village's chests.
2. At dawn, each household that's due takes one from the **village store** (the chests by the kitchens and
   Storehouses). The villagers are "enjoying their Berry Wine" in their mood for a while.
3. The hall checks the classes right after, so a luxury taken that dawn counts that day.

The food icon on the [Village Hall](village-hall.md) lists the luxuries in store, and **What next?** says when a class
is short of one ("Get Fine Clothes made and into the store: they take one at dawn").

You can put luxuries in the store yourself too: buy them from the makers, or from anyone who has them. Players can
drink the wines and read the papers.

## How it fits with the rest

- **What feeds it:** the four makers, and what feeds them: orchards and berry farms, sheep and leather, paper and ink,
  amethyst, copper, gold and emeralds.
- **What it affects:** who can rise from [Artisan](artisan.md) to [Burgher](burgher.md) to [Noble](noble.md), the
  village's [treasury](treasury.md) through the wants, and villagers' [moods](moods.md).
- **What breaks it:** an empty store. Berry Wine and Vintage Wine go every 2 days, so a Vintner who runs out of bottles
  is felt within the week. In the first 7 days after classes switch on, the village settles in: luxuries are taken but
  not yet needed.
