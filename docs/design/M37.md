# M37 design note: Furnished homes and modded storage (2.1)

Written for ROADMAP 37.1 (2026-10-17, lane c). The roadmap items 37.2 to 37.11 hold the full specs and their tests;
this note is the overview the owner reviews and the map the lanes build from. Lanes don't wait for the owner's reply;
anything he changes goes back into the items.

**In one line:** when the server has the pack's furniture mods, builders furnish what they build with them (Handcrafted
chairs round a Handcrafted table, Cozy Home sofas and clocks, CobbleFurnies Poké Ball chairs, Beautify blinds and
candelabras), villagers sit on the chairs and sleep in the fancy beds, and the Storehouse treats a Sophisticated
Storage controller or a Tom's Simple Storage connector as one big store; with none of those mods, nothing changes.

## What you'll see (the short version)

Every piece of furniture our builds draw today in vanilla blocks (a stair as a chair, a fence and pressure plate as a
table, a trapdoor as a cupboard door, a lantern, a red bed) becomes a **furniture spot**. When a builder starts a
build, each spot becomes the matching piece of the first furniture mod (in the order of `furnitureMods`) that the
server has; where no installed mod has that kind of piece, or the mod's piece doesn't fit, the vanilla blocks stay.

| Installed | A Starter Cottage looks like |
|---|---|
| **None** (plain Fabric, or a pack without them) | Exactly today's build, block for block. The 37.4 room layouts are drawn so they read as rooms in vanilla too. |
| **Handcrafted** | Handcrafted oak chairs round a Handcrafted oak table with its tablecloth, a fancy bed with a nightstand on each side, a cupboard, shelves with plates and cups, a couch in the sitting corner. Cushions and sheets take the colour the vanilla bed had. |
| **Lucky's Cozy Home** | Cozy Home chairs and table, a sofa, a grandfather clock in the hall, a wall clock and a table lamp. (Checked on the real pack only: the mod can't load in our dev runtime.) |
| **CobbleFurnies** | Wooden chairs and table, a nightstand and a cabinet; colour pieces (sofas, stools, lamps, curtains) where the vanilla piece was coloured; a Poké Ball chair and desk in a Trainer's room. |
| **Beautify** | Only what Handcrafted, Cozy Home and CobbleFurnies don't make: blinds at the windows, picture frames on the walls, candelabras and jar lamps, hanging pots, trellises and book stacks. |
| **Several** | Each spot is taken by the first mod in `furnitureMods` that has its kind: with the default order a pack with all four gets Handcrafted seating and beds, Cozy Home clocks and lamps, and Beautify blinds and pictures. |
| **Carved Wood / Rechiseled** (37.7) | Two new blueprint styles: a spruce build's chests, barrels, shelves and lanterns are spruce (Carved Wood), and the Grand style's floors and wall panels are patterned planks and bricks (Rechiseled). Without the mods those styles look like today's. |
| **Sophisticated Storage** (37.8, 37.9) | Its chests and barrels of every tier are supply chests, porters' chests and Storehouse chests; a barrel with a stack upgrade shows its real room on the Storehouse board; a controller in the Storehouse makes its linked blocks one store: "linked through a storage controller: 3 blocks". |
| **Tom's Simple Storage** (37.10) | An inventory connector in the Storehouse makes every chest on its cables one store; a terminal there lets a player see and take the village's goods with Tom's own screen. |

Villagers (37.6): chairs, stools, benches, couches and sofas of these mods are seats; villagers sit on them for meals
and in the evening and get up for work, danger and bed. Handcrafted's fancy beds are real beds, so villagers claim and
sleep in them and the house counts as their home. A furnished room counts towards M34's beauty need.

The Blueprint Table's materials list and the builder's shopping list show the pieces this server will build, under
their own names and icons (a Handcrafted Oak Chair, not an oak stair).

## Where it lives

| Part | Place |
|---|---|
| The flag and switches | `Expansions.M37`; `WorkplaceConfig` `furnishedBuilds`, `furnitureMods`, `moddedStorageNetworks`; applied to `furnish/Furnishings` and `store/StorageNetworks` (37.1) |
| Furniture spots | the generator (`tools/blueprints/`) writes `data/aliveworkplace/blueprint_furniture/<blueprint>.json` beside each template (37.2) |
| Furnishing tables | `data/aliveworkplace/furnishings/<mod>.json`, read by a reload listener through `Platform` (37.3) |
| The choice | `BuildPlan` picks each spot's piece when a build is planned; saved on the `BuildSite` (37.3) |
| Seats | block tag `aliveworkplace:villager_seats`, sitting as in the Stadium (`cup/StandSeat`) (37.6) |
| Styles | `data/aliveworkplace/blueprint_styles/` (37.7) |
| Storage networks | `store/StorageNetworks` with the Storehouse; network blocks found by walking block ids (37.9, 37.10) |

Everything is by **block and item ids, tags and data files**: no class of any of these mods is imported, compiled
against or checked with `isModLoaded`. A piece is used when `BuiltInRegistries.BLOCK` has its id; that is the whole
"is the mod there" test. So all of it is core code, and none of it is in `compat/`. The mods stay optional
(`suggests`).

## The furniture spot format (37.2)

One file per blueprint, `data/aliveworkplace/blueprint_furniture/<blueprint>.json`, written by `generate.py` for our
builds; a datapack can add one for its own builds. Positions are template coordinates (front is z = 0, y = 0 the
first layer). `blocks` are the vanilla blocks the piece is drawn with, relative to `pos`; `generate.py` checks each one
is the block in the template there.

Example, `data/aliveworkplace/blueprint_furniture/starter_cottage.json` (two of its spots):

```json
{
  "spots": [
    {
      "kind": "chair",
      "pos": [3, 1, 4],
      "facing": "south",
      "wood": "spruce",
      "blocks": [
        {"at": [0, 0, 0], "state": "minecraft:spruce_stairs[facing=north,half=bottom,shape=straight]"}
      ]
    },
    {
      "kind": "table",
      "pos": [3, 1, 5],
      "facing": "north",
      "wood": "spruce",
      "blocks": [
        {"at": [0, 0, 0], "state": "minecraft:spruce_fence"},
        {"at": [0, 1, 0], "state": "minecraft:white_carpet"}
      ]
    },
    {
      "kind": "bed",
      "pos": [1, 1, 6],
      "facing": "east",
      "wood": "spruce",
      "colour": "red",
      "blocks": [
        {"at": [0, 0, 0], "state": "minecraft:red_bed[facing=east,part=foot]"},
        {"at": [1, 0, 0], "state": "minecraft:red_bed[facing=east,part=head]"}
      ]
    }
  ]
}
```

- `kind`: one of the kinds below. `facing`: the way a person using the piece faces (a chair's sitter faces the table,
  a picture faces into the room; for a bed, the way from its foot to its head, as vanilla's bed `facing`; a table, which has no front, says north). `wood` (one of the eleven vanilla woods) or
  `colour` (one of the sixteen dyes), or both; a style carries them over (a spruce chair in the Dark Oak style is a
  dark oak chair, 37.2).
- As built (37.2): `tools/blueprints/furniture.py` finds chairs (a stair seat facing a table), tables (a lone post or a
  row of posts with a cloth or plate on it, or a top slab on a lone post) and beds in every finished drawing, and a
  drawing records the other kinds itself with `Build.furniture(kind, x, y, z, facing, cells, wood, colour)`; a spot
  without its own wood takes the wood most of the build is made of. 223 of our blueprints have spots files.
- The **footprint** is the set of `blocks` cells: the modded piece replaces exactly those cells (the ones it doesn't
  fill become air).
- A spot whose blocks don't match the template, or a file that doesn't parse, is logged with its path and skipped; the
  build is then vanilla there.

## The kinds, and each mod's pieces

Ids are from the jars in the pack (`build/packtest/server/mods`: Handcrafted 4.0.3, Lucky's Cozyhome Refurnished
1.1.20, CobbleFurnies 1.2, Beautify 2.0.0). `{wood}` is one of acacia, bamboo, birch, cherry, crimson, dark_oak,
jungle, mangrove, oak, spruce, warped (every wood all three furniture mods share; Cozy Home also has glass, iron,
ominous and undead, CobbleFurnies apricorn and saccharine, used only by a style that asks for them). `{colour}` is a
dye colour. A dash: that mod has no such piece, so the next mod in the order is tried, then vanilla.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| stool | slab, fence post | - | - | `cobblefurnies:{colour}_stool` | - |

| table | fence + carpet or pressure plate, top slab | `handcrafted:{wood}_table` | `cozyhome:{wood}_table` | `cobblefurnies:{wood}_table` | - |
| side table | top slab, barrel | `handcrafted:{wood}_side_table` | - | - | - |

| blinds | glass pane, trapdoor | - | - | `cobblefurnies:{colour}_curtain` | `beautify:{wood}_blinds` |
| crockery | flower pot, candle on a table | `handcrafted:{colour}_crockery_combo`, `_plate`, `_cup`, `_bowl` (white, blue, yellow, terracotta, wood) | - | - | - |

Pieces that are **never** used for a spot: anything that holds items (a modded cupboard or drawer is a decoration
here, not a store: `cozyhome:*_storage_counter` and CobbleFurnies cabinets have inventories, so they're placed only
where the vanilla piece had none, and builders never copy contents into them), job blocks (37.5), and pieces with an
entity or a gadget (`cozyhome:telescope`, `cobblefurnies:tv`, the fridges and toilets).

Checked from the jars: Handcrafted's seat and table blocks carry `color` (the cushion or cloth) and `facing`;
`handcrafted:*_fancy_bed` has `part=foot|head` like a vanilla bed and is in `#minecraft:beds`; CobbleFurnies chairs
and sofas carry `facing` (and sofas `type=single|left|middle|right`); Beautify blinds carry `open`, `facing`, `hidden`,
candelabras `on`, `facing`, `hanging`; Cozy Home's blockstates have a single variant (their look is a block-entity
model), so their facing is set from the block's own `facing` property where it has one, which the pack test checks.

## The furnishing table format (37.3)

One file per mod, `data/aliveworkplace/furnishings/<mod>.json`; the file's name is the mod namespace that
`furnitureMods` names. A datapack can add a file for another furniture mod and put its name in `furnitureMods`.

Example, `data/aliveworkplace/furnishings/handcrafted.json` (three of its kinds):

```json
{
  "pieces": {
    "chair": {
      "block": "handcrafted:{wood}_chair",
      "facing": "front",
      "states": {"color": "{colour}"},
      "colour": "white"
    },
    "table": {
      "block": "handcrafted:{wood}_table",
      "states": {"color": "{colour}"},
      "colour": "white"
    },
    "bed": {
      "block": "handcrafted:{wood}_fancy_bed",
      "facing": "front",
      "states": {"color": "{colour}"},
      "footprint": [
        {"at": [0, 0, 0], "states": {"part": "foot"}},
        {"at": [0, 0, 1], "states": {"part": "head"}}
      ]
    }
  }
}
```

- `block`: the id, with `{wood}` and `{colour}` filled from the spot. If the resulting id isn't in the block
  registry, this mod has no such piece for that wood or colour, and the next mod is tried.
- `facing`: how the piece's `facing` property relates to the spot's: `front` (the same), `back` (opposite), `left`,
  `right`; absent: the piece has no facing. `states`: other properties to set (unknown properties are ignored, so a
  table entry works across the mod's versions). `colour`: the colour used when the spot has none.
- `footprint`: the cells the piece fills, relative to the spot's `pos` with z pointing the spot's `facing`, each with
  its own states (the second half of a two-block piece); absent: one cell. The piece is used only if every cell of its
  footprint is a cell of the spot's `blocks`; otherwise that spot stays vanilla (37.3: "a spot whose modded footprint
  doesn't fit stays vanilla").
- A file with a mistake is logged with its path and skipped (the other mods' files still load).

Which builds get furnished rooms:

- **Homes (37.4):** the Starter Cottage, Stone House, Townhouse and Manor at all four levels and in every style, the
  M34 grander homes when M34 is on, and the Inn: a dining set round a table, a bedroom with nightstands and a lamp, a
  sitting corner, shelves of crockery, clocks and pictures on the walls, blinds at the windows.
- **Workplaces (37.5):** shops, the Library, the Healing Center, the Schoolhouse, the Village Hall, the Kitchen and the
  M34 workshops: desks for the Scribe and Printer, counters and shelves in shops, crockery and benches in the Kitchen,
  Beautify trellises, hanging pots and picture frames as decoration. Never a job block, a bed a villager needs, or a
  container (a GameTest checks no spot covers one).
- Everything else (farms, walls, Wonders, camps, Pokémon buildings) keeps its vanilla furniture until a later item
  adds spots to it; their templates have no spots file.

## The storage rules (37.8 to 37.10)

1. **Sophisticated chests and barrels are containers** (37.8): every tier of `sophisticatedstorage:chest`, `barrel`,
   `copper_`, `iron_`, `gold_`, `diamond_`, `netherite_` and `limited_*barrel_1` to `_4` is a supply chest, a porter's
   chest and a Storehouse chest (read and filled through Fabric's transfer API, `ItemStores`). They're in `#c:chests` /
   `#c:barrels`, so builders never break them while clearing and never copy them. The Storehouse's free room is
   counted in **items** where a slot holds more than 64 (a stack upgrade), not in slots
   (`SupplyContainers.freeSlots` counts slots today).
2. **A Sophisticated controller is one store** (37.9), with `moddedStorageNetworks` on: a
   `sophisticatedstorage:controller` in the Storehouse's area stands for every storage block linked to it (touching
   storage blocks and `storage_link`s, found by walking block ids within the controller's reach); those blocks drop out
   of the store list so nothing counts twice. First 37.9 checks the controller exposes item storage through the
   transfer API; if it doesn't, linked blocks stay separate stores (as today) and this note says so.
3. **A Tom's inventory connector is one store** (37.10), with `moddedStorageNetworks` on: a
   `toms_storage:inventory_connector` in the Storehouse's area stands for the inventories touching it and on its
   `inventory_cable`, `inventory_cable_connector` and framed cables; those drop out of the list. A `storage_terminal`
   or `crafting_terminal` lets a player use Tom's own screen on it. Filing cabinets stay stores of their own.
4. **Never twice:** `build/ModdedBlocks.STORAGE_NETWORK` keeps the network blocks (controllers, links, I/O blocks,
   every Tom's block but the filing cabinet) out of the supply chests everywhere else (porters, builders, every job
   that reads chests), as today. Only the Storehouse, with the switch on, reads a network through its head block.
5. With `moddedStorageNetworks` off, or the mod absent, every chest is its own store, as at 2.0.

## Config switches (`config/aliveworkplace.json`)

| Switch | Default | Screen | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| `furnishedBuilds` | on (off while M37 is unfinished) | yes | Builds use the installed furniture mods. Off: every build is the vanilla build (a site planned while it was on keeps its saved choice). |
| `furnitureMods` | `["handcrafted", "cozyhome", "cobblefurnies", "beautify"]` | file only (a list) | The order mods are tried in for each spot. Entries are trimmed and lower-cased, blanks and repeats dropped; an empty list means no mod (vanilla); a missing list takes the default. A name with no furnishing table is passed over. |
| `moddedStorageNetworks` | on (off while M37 is unfinished) | yes | Controllers and connectors in the Storehouse count as one store. Off: each linked chest is its own store. |

**The flag:** `Expansions.M37` (false on `main` until the release that completes the milestone), in `flag()`,
`MILESTONES` and `milestone()` for all three switches, so `ExpansionGateGameTests` covers them: with the gate closed a
config file can't turn them on and they're hidden from the settings screen. `FurnishingConfigGameTests` tests the
gate and the list's cleaning.

## Save data (every field optional with a default, so old saves load unchanged; nothing renamed)

| Where | Field | Default | Item |
|---|---|---|---|
| `BuildSite` | `furnishings`: per spot index, the chosen piece (`mod`, block state of each cell) | absent = every spot vanilla | 37.3 |
| `config` | `furnishedBuilds`, `furnitureMods`, `moddedStorageNetworks` | as above | 37.1 |

Nothing else is saved: a villager's seat is not saved (a villager sitting when the world is saved stands up after a
load, as Stadium spectators do); the store list and networks are found again on every Storehouse scan; the furnishing
tables and spots are data files.

## How each part falls back

| Missing | What happens |
|---|---|
| Every furniture mod | No spot finds a piece: every build is today's vanilla build, the materials list unchanged (a GameTest without mods proves it, 37.3). |
| One mod of the list | The next mod in `furnitureMods` that has the kind; else vanilla. |
| A mod removed after a build was planned | The site's saved choice holds while it is built; a piece whose block id is gone is placed as its vanilla blocks. Upkeep (`Upkeep`) puts the vanilla piece back where a modded one vanished on a finished build. |
| A spots file or table file with a mistake | Logged with its path and skipped; those spots stay vanilla. |
| `#aliveworkplace:villager_seats` modded entries | Written `"required": false`, so the tag loads without the mods (a GameTest, 37.6); vanilla stair seats aren't in it. |
| Carved Wood / Rechiseled | A style rule applies only where its result is a real block, so the new styles build as today's (37.7). A Carved Wood job block is swapped in only where Carved Wood registers it as the same POI. |
| Sophisticated controller with no transfer API storage | Linked blocks stay separate stores (37.9 checks first). |
| Sophisticated / Tom's absent | Nothing to read; the Storehouse works with vanilla chests as today. |
| Lucky's Cozy Home in the dev runtime | It can't load there (its `getItems()` clashes with a Mojang-mapped name), so its pieces are tested by the pack test only (37.11). |

## Other decisions taken (not the owner's)

- **No `compat/` package for these mods.** Ids, tags and data files only, as the milestone says; the registry lookup is
  the presence test. This keeps it all in core and portable.
- **Beautify last by default** because it makes decorations only; it never competes with the other three for seats,
  tables or beds, so its place in the order matters little.
- **Modded storage furniture is decoration only.** A Cozy Home storage counter or a CobbleFurnies cabinet may stand
  where a vanilla cupboard was drawn without an inventory, but nothing is ever put in it and no job reads it.
- **Cozy Home's sofa over its couch** for the couch kind: the sofa is the piece the milestone names.
- **The review package**: per `docs/agent/sessions.md` ("Review packages", 2026-10-04) lanes don't make packages; the
  digest sends this note to the owner as the item's package.
