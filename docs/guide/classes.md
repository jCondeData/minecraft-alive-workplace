In a village with a Village Hall, every household lives as a **class**: [Peasant](peasant.md),
[Artisan](artisan.md), [Burgher](burgher.md) or [Noble](noble.md). Give a household what the next class needs and it
rises in the world; let it go without what its own class needs and it comes down again.

![Peasant, Artisan, Burgher, Noble, left to right](https://jcondedata.github.io/minecraft-alive-workplace/class_outfits/still-1.jpg)

## Rising and falling

A household is one grown villager, or a married couple. Every dawn the hall looks at each one. A household that has
had everything the **next** class needs for **two dawns running** rises: golden sparkles and a chime at their door, a
line in chat for players nearby and in the chronicle, and a good mood for a couple of days ("rose in the world"). One
that has gone without something its **own** class needs for **three dawns running** comes down a class, and is unhappy
about it. Nobody falls below Peasant. Children take the class of the grown-ups they live with.

A villager who has what their class needs is a little happier; each need they lack makes them less happy.

![Odo and Pia rise to Artisan at dawn](https://jcondedata.github.io/minecraft-alive-workplace/classes/still-1.jpg)

## What the classes need and give

| Class | Needs | Gives |
|---|---|---|
| [Peasant](peasant.md) | nothing | the usual tax |
| [Artisan](artisan.md) | a home a builder built, fed 3 days, a varied diet, a chapel, school or clinic near home, Work Clothes | one and a half times the tax; crafters 10% faster; opens the Artisan jobs |
| [Burgher](burgher.md) | a tier II home, fed 3 days, a varied diet, a school and one more service, a Market Square, Fine Clothes, Berry Wine, the Gazette | two and a half times the tax; faster research; more caravans and traders |
| [Noble](noble.md) | a tier III home, fed 3 days, a varied diet, chapel, school, clinic and library, a Town, decorations, Noble Robes, Vintage Wine, an Emerald Brooch | four times the tax; a happier village; the Noble's Ball |

Each class also has **wants**: not needed, but every want a household has makes it pay 10% more tax.

## Seeing it at the hall

The **Classes** tab on the Village Hall counts the households of each class. Its page has a button per class, with each
need and how many households have it ("A varied diet: 2 of 4"), what the class gives, and the households closest to
rising with what they still lack. The people list shows each villager's class and ticks their needs, and "What next?"
gives tips ("2 Peasant households want A varied diet to rise to Artisan").

![The Burgher button: needs counted, what it gives, who's closest](https://jcondedata.github.io/minecraft-alive-workplace/classes/still-3.jpg)

## Dressed by class

You can tell a class at a glance: Artisans wear a blue neckerchief and a belt pouch, Burghers a dark frock coat with
brass buttons, Nobles a crimson mantle with an ermine collar and a gold chain, all over their job's outfit. Peasants
wear just their job's clothes.

## Higher jobs need higher classes

Some jobs need a household of a higher class: see [Artisan](artisan.md) and [Burgher](burgher.md). The jobs of plain
Minecraft villagers are open to everyone, and **nobody is ever fired**: a worker below their job's class keeps it, and
the hall's list marks them.

## Luxuries and the workers who make them

The better classes need **luxuries**, taken from the village store at dawn when due: the **Vintner** (a cauldron)
presses Cider, Berry Wine and Vintage Wine; the **Tailor** (a loom) sews Work Clothes, Fine Clothes and Noble Robes;
the **Printer** (a cartography table) prints the Village Gazette and the Illuminated Book; the **Jeweler** (a
stonecutter) makes the Amethyst Ring, the Emerald Brooch and the Gold Circlet. Keep them supplied and your households
climb.

## When classes first switch on

The first time a village has classes, everyone gets one in its first minute or two, from what they already have (never
Noble: that's earned), and the chronicle says how the people "found their places". For the next 7 days the village
**settles in**: nobody falls a class and luxuries aren't needed yet, so you have a week to set up your workshops. Nobody
loses a job, and a Peasant pays exactly the tax everyone paid before, so no village takes in less.
